\subsection{Design Decisions}

\paragraph{Why a new application-layer protocol?} Off-the-shelf transports
(ROS~2/DDS with default QoS, plain gRPC, raw TCP) do not differentiate between
a 20~Hz pose packet and a 3~MB submap upload. The result is either head-of-line
blocking (TCP), lack of deadline awareness (DDS best-effort), or no compression
integration. Co-LIC2's protocol is a thin shim ($\sim$2000 lines of C++)
that adds exactly the differentiation needed for cooperative SLAM and can be
deployed alongside existing middleware rather than replacing it.

\paragraph{Why cluster-head election instead of a static topology?} Static
topologies fail in mobile deployments: a pre-designated head may move behind a
building, losing 50\% of its members. Our election protocol adapts to
topology changes on a $\sim$3~s timescale, which is tolerable given that submap
publication is $\sim$3~s and map merge is $\sim$2~s. The cost (election messages,
complexity) is small relative to the robustness gain.

\paragraph{Why NPV as the compression objective?} Perceptual metrics (PSNR,
SSIM, LPIPS) require rendering---too expensive for per-submap optimization on
an edge GPU already running real-time SLAM ($\sim$15~ms per render at 720p).
NPV is computed in $\sim$1~ms from Gaussian parameters alone and empirically
correlates with rendering quality ($\rho=0.89$ in outdoor scenes). It is a
pragmatic proxy that the budget controller can use in its fast loop.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Static-scene assumption.} The per-node front-end inherits
Gaussian-LIC2's static-scene assumption. The confidence-weighted conflict
resolution (Eq.~\ref{eq:confidence}) is a first-pass treatment; it handles
slowly changing scenes (parked cars, construction progress) but not real-time
dynamic-object tracking. An integration with RoSe-SLAM~\cite{wang2026roseslam}-style
motion segmentation is the most important next step.

\paragraph{Wi-Fi~6 EDCA dependence.} Our QoS mapping assumes 802.11e EDCA
queues, which require WMM support on the AP. In unmanaged ad-hoc (IBSS) mode,
EDCA is optional, and in some 5G NR deployments per-flow QoS requires network
slice configuration. The protocol can function without EDCA (all streams
mapped to AC\_BE), but latency bounds degrade proportionally.

\paragraph{No cryptographic confidentiality.} The protocol authenticates nodes
via signed descriptors and submaps (Ed25519), but does not encrypt traffic. A
deployment in adversarial environments needs TLS or application-layer
encryption over the submap and keyframe streams.

\paragraph{Head scalability ceiling.} A single cluster head handles $\sim$8
members at $\sim$17~Mbps ingress. Beyond this, we spawn additional clusters;
cross-cluster consistency then depends on the Tier-0 server or opportunistic
head-to-head exchange, both of which have higher latency than intra-cluster
merge. The practical limit is $\sim$32 nodes with 4 heads + 1 server.

\subsection{Open Questions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dynamic cluster splitting/merging.} When a cluster grows beyond
    8 members (nodes join after election), should the head split the cluster
    or degrade QoS? A split protocol is complex but preserves latency bounds.
  \item \textbf{Energy-optimal compression on UAVs.} For battery-constrained UAV
    nodes, the trade-off between local compression (GPU energy) and
    transmission (radio energy) is deployment-specific. A joint energy model
    could push the budget controller toward coarser submaps for UAVs.
  \item \textbf{5G NR sliced-network measurement study.} Our 5QI mapping
    (Section~\ref{sec:channel}) is based on 3GPP specifications; a measurement
    campaign on a real 5G SA network would validate the QoS assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Multi-node dynamic-object consensus.} Per-node dynamic filtering
    plus cross-node voting to agree on the static subset of the map,
    required for long-term deployment in urban environments.
\end{itemize}
