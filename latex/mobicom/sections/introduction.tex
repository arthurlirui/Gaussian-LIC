% ============================================
% Section: Introduction
% ============================================

Cooperative autonomous systems---from multi-robot construction teams to
UAV--UGV disaster-response squads---require a shared, up-to-date 3D model of
their environment to coordinate navigation, planning, and task allocation.
Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} have
enabled real-time, photo-realistic mapping from a single mobile
platform~\cite{lang2026gaussian2,lang2025gaussian,xie2025gslivm,park2026livegs},
and multi-agent Gaussian SLAM is an active research
frontier~\cite{deambrogi2026cgsslam_mob,thomas2025grandslam_mob,deng2025mneslam}.

Yet a critical gap remains: \textbf{no existing system addresses the wireless
communication challenge that arises when multiple nodes share photo-realistic
Gaussian maps over bandwidth-limited, lossy wireless channels.} A typical
submap from a node traveling 5~m contains $5\times10^4$--$10^5$~Gaussians.
Even under aggressive compression, a single submap consumes 2--5~MB; a cluster
of eight nodes publishing every 3~s generates 5--13~Mbps of aggregate uplink
to the cluster head. Over Wi-Fi~6 or 5G~NR, this is manageable; over degraded
links---congested channels, edge-cell handover, ad-hoc mesh---submap delivery
fails, registrations stall, and the global map diverges.

This paper presents \textbf{Co-LIC2}, a cooperative multi-node
LiDAR-inertial-visual Gaussian SLAM system whose central contribution is a
purpose-built wireless communication architecture that jointly addresses
\emph{data representation}, \emph{protocol design}, and \emph{network topology}
for photo-realistic map sharing. Co-LIC2 extends the single-platform
Gaussian-LIC2 framework~\cite{lang2026gaussian2} along four axes, listed below
in decreasing order of novelty to the mobile computing community:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A QoS-aware multi-stream wireless protocol}
  (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}) that classifies traffic into four
  streams---real-time pose, bulk submap, reference keyframe, and control---and
  assigns differentiated 802.11e EDCA parameters, application-layer FEC, and
  deadline-aware scheduling. This is the core systems contribution: a protocol
  that can be deployed on commodity Wi-Fi~6/6E radios without modifying the
  MAC layer.

  \item \textbf{A submap-aware adaptive compression scheduler}
  (Section~\ref{sec:compression}) that jointly optimizes spherical-harmonics
  degree, spatial quantization step, and entropy-coding level under a hard
  bandwidth cap, maximizing view-dependent rendering quality through a novel
  perceptually motivated \emph{Number-of-Gaussians-Per-View} (NPV) metric.

  \item \textbf{A hierarchical hybrid network architecture}
  (Section~\ref{sec:network}) that combines stable cluster-head election,
  multi-hop submap routing, and three graceful degradation modes (full
  cooperation $\to$ intra-cluster P2P $\to$ autonomous single-node) to
  maintain map consistency under intermittent connectivity and node churn.

  \item \textbf{A cooperative pose-graph SLAM back-end}
  (Section~\ref{sec:mapping}) that registers, merges, and globally optimizes
  ownership-tagged Gaussian submaps from heterogeneous nodes with
  confidence-weighted conflict resolution.
\end{enumerate}

Co-LIC2 additionally contributes a \textbf{multi-sensor joint calibration
pipeline} (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) surveying recent efficient
target-based and targetless methods, and a \textbf{reference hardware design}
(Section~\ref{sec:hardware}) specifying a modular sensor pod with four global-
shutter cameras, two non-repetitive LiDARs, dual IMUs, and GPS-
disciplined time synchronization---the target platform for our ongoing
multi-rig evaluation.

We provide a complete protocol specification with finite-state machines, a
submap binary format, analytical latency and scalability bounds, and a
failure-mode recovery analysis. Co-LIC2 targets 2--32 heterogeneous nodes
with per-node uplink as low as 3.2~Mbps over Wi-Fi~6 and per-cluster submap
merge latency under 2~s, degrading gracefully to single-node Gaussian-LIC2
under total partition. Empirical evaluation on physical multi-rig hardware
is ongoing; all measured quantities reported in this draft are
\textcolor{red}{placeholder targets} to be replaced with hardware results.
