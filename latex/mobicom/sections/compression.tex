% ============================================
% Section: Adaptive Submap Compression and Scheduling
% ============================================
\label{sec:compression}

The submap compressor bridges the SLAM pipeline and the wireless protocol.
It must produce the smallest possible byte stream under a bandwidth budget
while preserving the rendering quality that makes Gaussian maps useful.

\subsection{Submap Extraction}

A submap $\submap_i$ is a spatially bounded subset of $\GaussianMap_i$,
exported when the node's travel distance since the last submap exceeds
$S_{\text{sub}}$ or elapsed time exceeds $T_{\text{sub}}$ (defaults: 5~m,
3~s). The bounding box is the axis-aligned convex hull of all Gaussians
created within the interval, padded by $1.5\times$ the maximum Gaussian
scale for border continuity. Each submap is identified by a globally unique
ID: $\text{node\_id}\ll 32\mid\text{seq}$.

\subsection{NPV Quality Metric}

\label{sec:npv}

We propose the \emph{Number-of-Gaussians-Per-View} (NPV) as a compression-
aware quality proxy computable from Gaussian parameters alone, avoiding
the cost of rendering:

\begin{equation}
  \Npv(\submap) = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V}\sum_{g\in\submap}
    \mathbf{1}\bigl[\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_g-\mathbf{p}_v\|<r_{\text{vis}}
    \land \alpha_g>\alpha_{\min}\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_g$, $\alpha_g$ are the Gaussian mean and opacity,
$r_{\text{vis}}$ is the visibility range (default 30~m), and $\mathbf{p}_v$
are $V$ viewpoint positions sampled on a sphere centered at the submap
centroid. Empirically, $\Npv\ge 5000$ corresponds to PSNR $\ge 28$~dB in
outdoor scenes; we validate this correlation in Section~\ref{sec:render}.

\subsection{Compression Knobs}

Four independently controllable knobs trade size against quality:

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Compression knobs (post-entropy-coding).}
  \label{tab:knobs}
  \footnotesize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.8cm}p{0.9cm}p{1.1cm}p{3.6cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    Knob & Sym. & Range & Effect \\
    \midrule
    SH degree      & $d$   & 0--3   & $\frac{24}{8(1+d)}$ B/G \\
    Opacity prune  & $\alpha_{\min}$ & 0.005--0.05 & drops low-$\alpha$ G \\
    Spatial quant.\@& $\Delta q$ & 1--5 cm & adds MSE $\Delta q^2/12$ \\
    Entropy code   & $l_{\text{zstd}}$ & 1--9 & 2--4$\times$ compr.\@ ratio \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The compressed submap size is $S_{\text{sub}}=|\GaussianMap_{\text{vis}}|\cdot
c_{\text{B/G}}(d,l_{\text{zstd}})$, where $c_{\text{B/G}}\approx 24$~B at
SH degree~2 with zstd-5.

\subsection{Joint Optimization}

For each submap, the compressor solves:

\begin{equation}
  \max_{d,\alpha_{\min},\Delta q,l_{\text{zstd}}} \Npv\bigl(g(d,\alpha_{\min},\Delta q)\bigr)
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad S_{\text{sub}}\le S_{\max},
  \label{eq:opt}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\max}=\Bbudget/f_s$. The search space is small ($4\times10\times5\times9
=1800$ points); we exhaustively enumerate with early termination, completing
in $<$1~ms on GPU.

\subsection{Budget Controller}

A feedback loop monitors a 5~s sliding window of uplink throughput. If
observed throughput exceeds $1.1\times\Bbudget$, aggressiveness increases
(lower SH degree, coarser quantization) for subsequent submaps. If
throughput drops below $0.7\times\Bbudget$ and $\Npv$ falls below 80\%
of baseline, the controller relaxes. Convergence occurs within 3--5 submap
cycles ($\approx$15~s).
