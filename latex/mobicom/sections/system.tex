% ============================================
% Section: System Model and Wireless Channel
% ============================================
\label{sec:system}

We formalize the node model, per-node multi-sensor fusion, the wireless
channel, and the traffic characterization that drives protocol design.

\subsection{Node Model}

Each node $i \in \{1,\dots,N\}$ is a mobile platform carrying $N_c^{(i)}\ge 1$
cameras (with known intrinsics, distortion, and resolution), $N_l^{(i)}\ge 1$
LiDARs (potentially of heterogeneous types---mechanical, solid-state, MEMS),
one virtual IMU fusing all physical IMUs at 200--400~Hz, an edge GPU ($\ge$20
TFLOPS), and a wireless NIC (Wi-Fi~6/6E or 5G~NR). All sensor intrinsics,
extrinsics $T_{B_i\leftarrow S}$, and time offsets $\Delta t_S$ relative to
the IMU are calibrated (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}) and persisted in the
signed node descriptor (Section~\ref{sec:descriptor}).

\subsection{Wireless Channel Model}

\label{sec:channel}

We model two deployment scenarios.\footnote{The protocol design
targets both; the scheduling and EDCA mapping are identical.}

\paragraph{Wi-Fi 6/6E.} Each node connects to one or more 802.11ax APs
($2\times2$ MIMO, 80~MHz). The per-link PHY rate at time $t$ depends on the
MCS index $m^{(i)}(t)$, spatial streams $S_{\text{ss}}$, and channel bandwidth
$W$:
\begin{equation}
  R_{\text{phy}}^{(i)}(t) = S_{\text{ss}} \, W \, \eta_{\text{code}}(m^{(i)}) \, \log_2 M(m^{(i)}).
\end{equation}
The effective MAC throughput, accounting for EDCA contention and A-MPDU
overhead, is $R_{\text{eff}}^{(i)}(t) = \alpha_{\text{MAC}} \, R_{\text{phy}}^{(i)}(t)$
with $\alpha_{\text{MAC}}\in[0.6,0.8]$. At MCS~11 (1024-QAM, 5/6 code rate),
$R_{\text{eff}}\approx 480$~Mbps; at MCS~0 (BPSK, 1/2) under deep fade,
$R_{\text{eff}}\approx 3$~Mbps---a $160\times$ dynamic range that the
protocol must accommodate.

\paragraph{5G NR.} Nodes connect via NR-U or licensed NR, using configured-
grant URLLC for pose streams and dynamic-grant eMBB for submap uploads.
Per-flow QoS is enforced via 5QI values (QoS~\ref{tab:traffic}).

\paragraph{Link quality estimator.} Each node maintains an EWMA of effective
throughput and packet delivery ratio (PDR):
\begin{equation}
  \bar{R}_{\text{eff}}^{(i)}[k] = 0.9\,\bar{R}_{\text{eff}}^{(i)}[k-1] + 0.1\,R_{\text{eff}}^{(i)}[k],
  \qquad
  \text{PDR}^{(i)} = \tfrac{\text{pkts\_acked}}{\text{pkts\_sent}}.
\end{equation}
The composite link quality $Q^{(i)} = \bar{R}_{\text{eff}}^{(i)}\cdot\text{PDR}^{(i)}$
feeds into cluster-head election (Section~\ref{sec:election}) and compression budget allocation
(Section~\ref{sec:compression}).

\subsection{Per-Node Continuous-Time Multi-Sensor Fusion}

\label{sec:ctfusion}

Each node estimates a non-uniform cubic B-spline trajectory
$\Twb^{(i)}(t)\in\SEthree$ using a generalized version of
Coco-LIC~\cite{lang2023coco} and Gaussian-LIC2~\cite{lang2026gaussian2}.
Control points are placed adaptively based on IMU acceleration magnitude.

\paragraph{IMU residuals.} For a measurement at $t_k$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}_{\text{IMU}}(t_k) =
  \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_k-\hat{\mathbf{a}}(t_k) \\
  \boldsymbol{\omega}_k-\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t_k) \end{bmatrix}_{\Sigma_{\text{IMU}}^{-1}},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{a}}$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}$ derive from the spline's
analytical derivatives.

\paragraph{LiDAR residuals.} For LiDAR $l$, point $\mathbf{p}_k^{(l)}$ at $t_k$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}_{\text{LiDAR}}^{(l)}(t_k) =
    \mathbf{n}^\top\bigl(\Twb^{(i)}(t_k)\,T_{B_i\leftarrow L_l}\,
    \mathbf{p}_k^{(l)}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_*\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol{\mu}_*$ the nearest Gaussian mean in $\GaussianMap_i$ and
$\mathbf{n}$ the local surface normal from surrounding Gaussian covariances.

\paragraph{Camera residuals.} For camera $c$, keyframe at $t_k$: an SSIM+L1
photometric residual compares the observed image against $\GaussianMap_i$
rendered at the spline-evaluated pose, as in Gaussian-LIC2.

\paragraph{Time-offset residuals.} Up to $N_c+N_l$ unknown offsets relative to
the IMU are jointly estimated with the spline in a sliding-window Gauss-Newton
solve with B-spline motion prior, yielding the per-node trajectory and local
Gaussian map $\GaussianMap_i$.
