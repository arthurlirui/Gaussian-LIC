% Section: Related Work
% ============================================

Co-LIC2 draws on four communities: (i) wireless QoS and multi-stream
protocols, (ii) edge-assisted collaborative SLAM, (iii) multi-agent Gaussian
and neural SLAM, and (iv) multi-sensor continuous-time odometry. We survey
each thread and position our contributions.

\subsection{Wireless QoS and Multi-Stream Protocols}

The IEEE 802.11e amendment~\cite{ieee80211e} defines EDCA with four access
categories---AC\_VO, AC\_VI, AC\_BE, AC\_BK---differentiated by AIFSN,
CWmin/max, and TXOP. Application-layer frameworks such as MPEG-DASH and
WebRTC leverage EDCA for real-time video, but \emph{no prior work
applies EDCA-aware scheduling to the multi-modal traffic mix of cooperative
SLAM}: low-latency pose telemetry, bursty submap uploads, keyframe references,
and lightweight control messages. Co-LIC2 maps these four traffic types to
EDCA queues and supplements them with application-layer FEC and deadline-aware
pacing.

Deadline-aware schedulers such as DRAGON~\cite{hou2023dragon} (NSDI~2023) and
PIAS~\cite{bai2015pias} (SIGCOMM~2015) target data-center or general streaming
workloads. AdaMap~\cite{liu2023adamap} provides bounded end-to-end latency
for cooperative perception at the edge via priority queues---the closest
prior to our scheduling philosophy---but addresses LiDAR point-cloud fusion
rather than Gaussian map exchange. Co-LIC2's scheduler simultaneously manages
four traffic streams with explicit per-stream QoS contracts and integrates
with a Gaussian-specific compression optimizer.

\subsection{Edge-Assisted Collaborative SLAM}

The mobile-computing community has produced the most systems-rigorous
collaborative SLAM work. \textbf{SwarmMap}~\cite{xu2022swarmmap} (NSDI~2022)
scales real-time collaborative visual SLAM via change-log-based server--client
synchronization and priority-aware scheduling, handling $2\times$ more agents
than prior art. \textbf{Map++}~\cite{zhang2024mapp} (MobiCom~2024) introduces
user-participatory SLAM with metadata-based overlap assessment, reducing
traffic by $\approx$46\%. \textbf{Covins}~\cite{schmuck2021covins} provides
a decentralized-frontend, centralized-backend VI-SLAM architecture, and
\textbf{Swarm-SLAM}~\cite{lajoie2024swarmslam} delivers a sparse, fully
decentralized framework. These systems validate the collaborative architecture
but target feature-point maps over relatively benign links; they neither
address the bandwidth and streaming challenges of photo-realistic Gaussian
maps nor design differentiated QoS for multi-modal traffic.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Gaussian and Neural SLAM}

Photo-realistic multi-agent SLAM is advancing rapidly.
\textbf{CGS-SLAM}~\cite{deambrogi2026cgsslam_mob} performs collaborative
Gaussian SLAM with metric monocular depth and VGGT-based alignment.
\textbf{GRAND-SLAM}~\cite{thomas2025grandslam_mob} achieves large-scale global
consistency through local optimization and loop closure.
\textbf{MNE-SLAM}~\cite{deng2025mneslam} (CVPR~2025) provides distributed
neural SLAM with intra-to-inter loop closure and releases the INS dataset.
\textbf{CP-SLAM}~\cite{hu2024cpslam} (NeurIPS~2024) is a collaborative neural
point-based system. These works establish feasibility but target RGB-D or
monocular agents and do not model wireless constraints. Co-LIC2 is the first
to carry LiDAR and IMU per node---for metric scale and textureless-region
robustness---and to design the wireless protocol as a first-class system
component.

\subsection{Multi-Sensor Continuous-Time Odometry}

The per-node front-end builds on \textbf{Coco-LIC}~\cite{lang2023coco_mob}
(continuous-time B-spline LIV odometry with online time-offset calibration)
and \textbf{Gaussian-LIC2}~\cite{lang2026gaussian2_mob} (Coco-LIC augmented
with 3DGS for real-time photo-realistic mapping).
\textbf{FAST-LIVO2}~\cite{zheng2024fastlivo2_mob} supports multi-camera and
multi-LiDAR configurations in a discrete-keyframe framework. Co-LIC2
generalizes Coco-LIC's residual stack to $N_c$ cameras and $N_l$ LiDARs per
node with online multi-offset estimation, which is essential for heterogeneous-
node deployments and provides the continuous-time trajectory from which
submaps are carved at arbitrary temporal boundaries.

\subsection{Positioning}

Prior work has addressed collaborative SLAM systems for feature-point maps
under real networks, and photo-realistic multi-agent Gaussian SLAM under
benign links. Co-LIC2 is the first system to design a \textbf{complete
wireless protocol stack}---QoS-aware multi-stream transport, deadline-driven
compression scheduling, and hierarchical graceful degradation---for
cooperative photo-realistic Gaussian SLAM.
